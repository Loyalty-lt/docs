# Email marketing

A partner writes a campaign in the Partners Portal, picks who gets it, and sends. This
page describes what happens after that — useful if you are integrating, debugging a
missing email, or explaining an invoice line.

Nothing here is something your integration calls. Marketing email is sent from the
Partners Portal, not over the Shop API.

## What a partner gets

**Campaigns** — one-off emails with a subject, HTML body, preview text and an audience
filter. Saved as a draft, scheduled, or sent immediately.

**Templates** — reusable emails. A partner sees their own plus the ones we prepare;
ours are read-only for them.

**Automations** — rules that fire on customer behaviour: inactivity, birthday, sign-up
anniversary, no purchase in N days, points about to expire, N days after sign-up. Each
rule has a frequency cap so the same person is not emailed twice for the same reason.

**Audiences** — named filter sets (inactive for N days, points range, signed up after a
date) saved once and reused on any campaign. A saved audience is a rule, not a list of
people: its size is recalculated, so it keeps up with the customer base.

**Consents and suppressions** — who may be emailed, and who may never be again.

**Reports** — opens, clicks, bounces, unsubscribes, top links, devices, and opens by
hour of day.

## Who receives a campaign

Every partner chooses one of two policies under **Email marketing → Consents**:

| Policy | Audience |
|--------|----------|
| `consent_required` (default) | Only customers with a recorded marketing consent for that partner |
| `opt_out` | Every customer of that partner who has an email address and has not unsubscribed |

On top of the policy, three groups are always excluded, under both policies:

- anyone who unsubscribed from that partner,
- any address that hard-bounced or produced a spam complaint — this one is
  platform-wide, because an address that does not exist does not exist for anyone,
- anyone without an email address.

The partner is the data controller for their customers. Choosing `opt_out` is a
statement that they have a lawful basis to email those people.

## What it costs

Marketing email counts against the `marketing_email` quota in the partner's plan
(Grow 10 000/month, Pro 50 000/month) and is billed per message above it. Transactional
mail — receipts, password resets, invoices — never counts.

The quota is checked once for the whole campaign before the first message goes out, not
per message. A campaign that would cross a hard stop the partner enabled is refused with
`402` and nothing is sent; see [Plan limits](/docs/api-reference/plan-limits).

## A/B testing subject lines

A campaign can carry a second subject line. When it does, an equal slice of the audience
(10–30%, the partner chooses) gets each one and the rest wait. After the window — 1 to 48
hours — the slice that collected more opens, or more clicks, decides which subject line
goes to everyone else.

The split is random, not by signup date: otherwise one branch gets the oldest customers
and the result measures the audience rather than the wording. A tie goes to the original
subject line. If the audience is too small for both branches to have recipients and still
leave someone for the winner, the campaign is sent normally with the first subject line
and no test is run.

The branch each message belonged to is recorded with the message, so the report shows
opens and clicks for both, not only for the metric that decided.

## Sending again to non-openers

A sent campaign can be re-sent to the people it reached who never opened it. This
produces a new draft, not a second send: the audience is pinned to the non-openers of
the original, and the partner is expected to rewrite the subject line before sending —
the same subject line to the same person performs worse the second time.

It requires open tracking on the original campaign. Without it "did not open" means
"everyone", which is a repeat, not a follow-up.

## Unsubscribe

Every marketing email carries:

- a visible unsubscribe link in the footer,
- `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` headers, so
  the recipient's mail client can offer its own one-click unsubscribe.

An unsubscribe is immediate, applies to that partner, and also revokes any consent record
— so a partner switching policy back to `consent_required` can never resurrect someone
who opted out.

## Bounces

The return path of every marketing message is `bounce+{token}@…`, so a bounce identifies
its original message without anyone parsing the body. Bounces are read from a dedicated
mailbox every ten minutes and classified by their RFC 3463 status:

| Status | Meaning | What happens |
|--------|---------|--------------|
| `5.x.x` | Permanent — no such address, blocked | Suppressed immediately, platform-wide |
| `4.x.x` | Temporary — mailbox full, greylisting | Counted; suppressed after five in a row |
| Feedback report | The recipient marked it as spam | Suppressed immediately |

If a campaign's bounce rate passes 5%, sending stops and the partner is told. One bad
address list should not cost everyone else their deliverability.

## Tracking

Opens are measured with a 1×1 pixel, clicks by rewriting links to a redirect. Both can be
turned off per campaign. A click redirect only ever resolves a link that was in the
campaign — the URL is never taken from the request, so the redirect cannot be borrowed to
make a phishing link look like ours. IP addresses are stored hashed, never in the clear.

Apple Mail Privacy Protection loads images for the recipient, so open rates are an
approximation everywhere, including here.

## Sending

Messages go out in batches of 100 with a pause between batches, capped per hour and per
recipient domain. This is deliberate: a burst from one IP is the fastest way into a spam
folder, and the volumes involved do not need more speed.
